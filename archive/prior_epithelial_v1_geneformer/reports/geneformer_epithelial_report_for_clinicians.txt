Geneformer Epithelial Cell Analysis Report
LUAD vs SCLC and LUAD-to-SCLC Transition

Prepared for: medical collaborators and research team
Input notebook: example_epithelial.ipynb

1. Executive Summary

This notebook performed a Geneformer analysis of lung-derived epithelial cells from a single-cell RNA-seq dataset containing lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), small cell lung carcinoma (SCLC), and normal lung samples.

The main epithelial analysis focused on malignant epithelial cells from LUAD and SCLC. The analysis asked whether Geneformer could distinguish LUAD epithelial transcriptional states from SCLC epithelial transcriptional states, and then used in silico gene overexpression to ask which genes would shift LUAD epithelial embeddings toward an SCLC-like state.

The workflow successfully:
- loaded the full lung cancer single-cell dataset;
- filtered it to lung epithelial cells;
- created a two-class LUAD vs SCLC epithelial subset;
- tokenized epithelial cell transcriptomes for Geneformer;
- fine-tuned a Geneformer cell classifier;
- extracted epithelial cell embeddings;
- performed all-gene in silico overexpression perturbation;
- generated ranked perturbation candidates for LUAD-to-SCLC state shift.

The strongest biological interpretation is that Geneformer detected a clear disease-associated transcriptional distinction between LUAD and SCLC epithelial cells. The LUAD-vs-SCLC epithelial classifier showed high validation performance, and perturbation analysis nominated several genes whose simulated overexpression shifted LUAD epithelial cells toward the SCLC goal state. These results are hypothesis-generating and should be interpreted with attention to donor imbalance, limited SCLC donor count, and the computational nature of perturbation.

Interpretation:

Important caveat:
The printed donor split for the three-class run showed donor IDs repeated across splits. For example, RU682 appeared in both train and eval, RU684 appeared more than once in train, and RU675 appeared in both train and test. This suggests possible donor leakage caused by donors having multiple disease labels in the donor-disease table. This three-class result should therefore be considered exploratory until strict donor-exclusive splitting is applied.

8. In Silico Perturbation

The perturbation analysis modeled an epithelial disease-state transition:
- start state: lung adenocarcinoma
- goal state: small cell lung carcinoma
- alternate states: none

Perturbation setup:
- perturbation type: overexpress
- genes perturbed: all detected genes
- model type: CellClassifier
- number of classes: 2
- maximum cells for perturbation: 2,500
- embedding layer: -1
- forward batch size: 256
- model version: V1

Meaning:
The analysis asked which simulated gene overexpression events move LUAD epithelial cells closer to the SCLC epithelial state in Geneformer embedding space. This is best interpreted as a computational screen for candidate genes or programs associated with SCLC-like transition, lineage plasticity, or LUAD/SCLC transcriptional divergence.

Interpretation of example hits:

These are computational candidates, not validated drivers. Genes with broad housekeeping roles or very high expression should be deprioritized unless supported by independent biological evidence.

10. Biological Interpretation

The epithelial analysis supports three preliminary conclusions:

1. LUAD and SCLC epithelial cells are strongly separable by Geneformer.
The two-class epithelial classifier achieved validation accuracy of 0.951 and macro-F1 of 0.941. This suggests robust tumor-intrinsic transcriptional differences between LUAD and SCLC epithelial cells.

2. Geneformer embeddings can support LUAD-to-SCLC transition hypotheses.
The perturbation analysis specifically modeled LUAD epithelial cells moving toward the SCLC epithelial state, which is aligned with questions about lineage plasticity and SCLC-like transformation.

3. Perturbation hits require biological filtering.
The significant list is large, with 1,059 genes passing FDR and detection filters. Many top genes are abundant epithelial or housekeeping genes. Prioritization should focus on genes with known relevance to lung epithelial lineage, neuroendocrine transition, cell-cycle state, MYC/TP53/RB1 biology, or SCLC transformation.

11. Limitations

Major limitations:
- The main LUAD/SCLC epithelial subset is imbalanced: 25,988 SCLC cells vs 5,270 LUAD cells.
- Donor counts are also imbalanced: 9 SCLC donors vs 16 LUAD donors.
- The model was trained for only one epoch.
- Geneformer V1 was used; a future run could compare V2.
- Hyperparameter optimization was not performed.
- Test metrics were not printed in the saved notebook output.
- The three-class epithelial classifier showed possible donor leakage and should not be treated as final.
- In silico perturbation is a computational prediction and requires experimental or orthogonal validation.
- Overexpression of all genes can surface highly expressed housekeeping genes; biological interpretation should apply additional filters.

13. Suggested Summary for Collaborators

We performed a preliminary Geneformer analysis of lung-derived epithelial cells from LUAD and SCLC single-cell RNA-seq data. After filtering to 31,258 LUAD/SCLC epithelial cells, a Geneformer classifier was trained to distinguish LUAD from SCLC epithelial states. The model showed strong validation performance, with accuracy of 0.951 and macro-F1 of 0.941, indicating that epithelial tumor cells contain robust disease-specific transcriptional signal. We then extracted Geneformer embeddings and performed an all-gene overexpression perturbation screen to identify genes whose simulated overexpression shifts LUAD epithelial cells toward an SCLC-like state. The perturbation screen identified 1,059 significant genes after FDR and detection filtering, including examples such as SFTPC, TPT1, SLPI, PTMA, and ACTB as positive-shift genes, and B2M, S100A9, FTH1, RALGAPA2, and ITGB8 as negative-shift genes. These findings are hypothesis-generating and should be rerun with strict donor-level splitting, explicit saved metrics, and biological prioritization focused on lung epithelial lineage and SCLC transformation.
